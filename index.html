Straight answers

The questions every electrician asks.

Which NEC edition is it based on?

The book is being built against the 2026 NEC — Texas already adopted it (effective Sept 1, 2026) and more states are moving. The current best-selling price book is still 2023-aligned.

Honest context: labor hours barely move between code cycles — what changes is which code-required items exist (GFCI/AFCI expansion, surge protection, and similar). And because adoption is staggered by state, the settings tab lets you adjust line items to your local amendments. A price book is labor math plus your market — the edition is the starting point, not the whole story.

What format is it? I don't want another app.

It's an Excel workbook — formula-driven throughout, and it opens in free Google Sheets. No app to install, no account, no subscription, works offline in the truck. If you've ever built your own spreadsheet for job costing, this will feel familiar — except someone already did the 250-line labor research for you.

When does it launch, and what will it cost?

Launch is planned soon — join the list above and you'll hear first, with early-buyer pricing for list members. The planned price is $99 one-time: no monthly fee, no per-tech licensing.

My market's rates aren't the book's rates. Then what?

That's exactly what the settings tab is for. Enter your labor rate, overhead, and margin targets once and every line reprices to your market. The book ships with 2026 national-average calibration as the starting point — your numbers replace it in about two minutes.

Copper prices change weekly. Won't the book go stale?

This is the #1 thing working electricians ask about — and it's why the book is built around the settings tab instead of fixed numbers. When your wire costs move, you update your material costs once and every affected line reprices. You're never locked to the prices from the day the book was written.

Planned from the start: the book ships calibrated to 2026 market rates, and the settings tab is the update mechanism — no new edition to buy when copper runs.

I already use field-service software. Why would I need this?

You might not — if your app's price book is already built out and your techs actually use it, you're set. This is for the shops paying $189/month largely for a price catalog they had to build themselves, or the shops still quoting from experience and a spreadsheet. $99 once vs. $2,268 a year is the whole argument.
